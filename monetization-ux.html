Technical Issues & UX friction

Store, purchase, and play-flow friction seen in recorded player sessions — the blockers that never reach the warehouse.

About the screens below. Screens are reconstructions of moments observed in recorded sessions. Actual client footage is withheld to preserve anonymity — in your pilot, findings ship with the real clips attached.
3.1

The near-miss wall has no pity mechanic

What happens on screen: the player reaches a timed chapter boss, plays the fight well, and loses to the timer with the boss at 1–5% HP ("TIME OVER" — a damage gap, not dying). They retry. And retry. Between some retries they upgrade (summons, gear-enhancement materials, gems); between others they change nothing. The screen says nothing about how to close the gap: no combat power target, no damage figure, no suggestion of what to raise. The game knows exactly how far short the player fell and never tells them, so they retry the same fight unchanged and fail the same way. Nor is there a bridging mechanic — no building buff, no mercy damage bonus, no "you were close" assist. After 6–14 straight near-misses the run ends one of three ways: an upgrade-then-retry loop, a pivot to low-stakes content, or a spend escalation into premium shop packs.

The give-up marker: the fail screen offers Retry and Leave. The moment a player taps Leave instead of Retry, the session's shape changes — Leave-players pivot to low-engagement activities (World Boss idle, Arena, Sleep Mode) and often do not come back to the boss.

Where seen: churned-cohort walls span Ch 22–30 plus Growth Dungeons; the same anatomy appears at Ch 2–5 for brand-new players (bounce cohort) and at Ch 7–31 for Segment 469 members.

Business cost: this is the trigger that starts most churn arcs on video (wall → spend → no payoff → fade). One pity/bridging mechanic addresses it at every level band at once.

Examples:

  1. Ch 23 Stage 7 — 11 consecutive fails, boss at 1% HP three or more times, no breakthrough.
  2. Ch 25 Stage 9 (Boss D) — 14 consecutive fails in one stretch; retries continue without any upgrade action between attempts.
  3. Ch 26 (Boss F) — fail → player taps Leave, then manually logs out via settings; one of the clearest filmed give-up moments.
  4. Ch 29 Stage 11 (Boss E) — a whale-tier churned player fails 3× at the same wall: spending relocates the wall, it does not remove it.
3.2

Store bug: errorCode 9-100302 "Unavailable in store"

What happens on screen: a player with visible intent to pay opens a real-money offer (premium-gem packs, memberships, hot deals), reaches checkout, and gets "Unavailable in store" (errorCode 9-100302). Within a single session the error repeats deterministically — the same offer fails every time — and players retry the same broken offer 2–3 times before giving up. Purchases in the affected cohorts span four different storefront currencies — this is not a single-storefront glitch.

Business cost: these are willing buyers blocked at the till. Warehouse framing for scale: 30,693 players had a failed/cancelled payment before their first-ever purchase (§3.9) — the recordings point to a store-side failure as one of the causes.

Examples:

  1. One player fired the same event hot-deal purchase 3× in one session — same 9-100302 error each time.
  2. Another hit the error on a Deluxe Membership, then tried Special Membership — blocked on both.
  3. A related vendor error (9-100204 "Vendor not initialized") appears in BOTH converting and abandoning sessions — successful buyers retry through it, window-shoppers stop; the error is the same, the need-state differs.
  4. Corroboration on a client-defined cohort: a Segment 469 member triggers a reward-ad purchase and gets an immediate "Unable to purchase" error (same purchase-blocking family).
3.3

The silent-success trust bug

What happens on screen: the player completes a purchase, the UI shows a failure screen (error codes 100315, 100107, 100204-24001) — and moments later the purchased item arrives in the mailbox anyway. The transaction succeeded; the interface said it failed.

Why it matters: a payment flow that reports false failures teaches players not to trust the store. The likely behaviors it trains — double-purchasing, refund requests, or refusing to buy again — are all bad outcomes, and none are visible in billing data (the order row looks normal).

Examples:

  1. "Failure" screen (codes 100315 / 100107 / 100204-24001) followed by the item landing in the mailbox in the same session — UI says fail, transaction completed.
  2. Independent sighting of the same error family in the exit-signal cohort: payment errors 9-100315 / 10-100107 during checkout in churned players' final sessions.
3.4

Ad errors that lie

What happens on screen: a player watches a rewarded ad to the end — and the game fires "The ad has not loaded." In the sharpest clip the error overlay persists behind a successful "Obtained Items" popup: the game simultaneously claims the ad failed and pays the reward. In another, one attempt produces a double error — "you skipped the ad" (red) plus "ad has not loaded" (yellow) — for a single watch. Skipping is separately punished with reward denial.

Why it matters: rewarded ads are the primary resource pipeline for non-spenders (issue 7). Errors that misreport what the player just did erode trust in the exact loop that keeps non-payers progressing — and they cluster at the churn-adjacent moments (post-failure, reward claim).

Examples:

  1. Ad completes visibly → "The ad has not loaded" fires anyway.
  2. Contradictory double state: error overlay persisting behind a successful "Obtained Items" popup.
  3. Double error on one attempt: "you skipped the ad" + "ad has not loaded" together; the monetization lens also caught a "you skipped the ad" reward denial mid-flow — players retried rather than abandoning.
3.5

Server-wait stacking

What happens on screen: "Waiting for server response" modals appear at predictable chokepoints — login, combat menus, party matching, friend list, guild screens, guild conquest, bulk summons, arena transitions. The modal blocks input while the game world keeps running underneath. The waits stack: one player hit 4 modals within 2 minutes.

Why it matters: no single wait is fatal; the stacking is. It lands on the highest- engagement surfaces (guild, arena, summons) — the content that retains social players.

Examples:

  1. Four consecutive "Waiting for server response" modals within 2 minutes for one player, input blocked each time.
  2. Waits observed at every UI layer for fully-churned players — login, combat menus, party matching, guild, friends.
  3. One churned player failed Ch 23 five times consecutively with server overlays stacking during the attempts — wall friction and server friction compounding in the same minutes.
3.6

"Session expired" crash right after boss failures

What happens on screen: the player fails a chapter boss twice in a row, and then — at their most frustrated moment — "Session has expired" fires, the loading screen freezes, and the run ends. On re-login, another server wait. Seen in four independent verified cohorts, so it is a pattern, not a one-off.

Why it matters: the crash lands precisely on the churn-adjacent moment (consecutive boss failure). For several filmed players it was the last meaningful event before the session dissolved into Sleep Mode or disconnection.

Examples:

  1. Two consecutive fails at Boss C (Ch 22) → session-expiration crash → frozen loading screen, run over.
  2. Same wall, different cohort: repeated near-miss fails at Ch 22 Boss C ending in a session-expiry error in a churned player's final sessions.
  3. A Minnow-tier spender's session collapses into idle after a near-miss wall plus a session-expiry error.
  4. Corroboration: "Session has expired" freezes recur in Segment 469 members' sessions.
3.7

Ad-loop friction on the players who depend on ads

What happens on screen: non-spending players chain 5–10+ rewarded-ad breaks per session across five touchpoints (shop currency, free summons, Quick Hunt, Daily Ad Booster, timer skips) — ads are their primary resource pipeline. Inside that loop: rewards denied on "skipped" ads, ad-cooldown blocks, ad-load failures — while players who bought the ad-removal package skip the entire tax. And the ad inventory itself is competitor marketing: 40+ rival titles shown at the player's most frustrated moments, with breaks running 15s–3min each.

Why it matters: the loop concentrates friction on exactly the segment with no wallet alternative — the game's most churn-fragile population — and monetizes their frustration with rivals' install ads.

Examples:

  1. A churned non-spender watches many full-screen ads for other games in one session, is denied a reward on a "skipped" ad, and then hits ad-cooldown blocks — while ad-removal buyers in the same cohort skip all of it.
  2. Ad breaks run 15 seconds to ~3 minutes with store-redirect end cards on nearly every ad; one tap-through to a rival's store page caught on film.
  3. "The ad has not loaded" failures 3–4× in a row blocking the zero-commitment monetization touchpoint for payment-cohort players.
3.8

The Arena "cannot generate recommendations" bug

What happens on screen: a player opens Arena — a prerequisite for main-quest advancement — and matchmaking never loads: the game "cannot generate recommendations." There is no workaround on screen. The player is hard-blocked on the main quest, files an in-game support ticket mid-session, and eventually logs out. Baseline expectation: Arena should load for every player who has unlocked it.

Where found: Segment 469 — a top-5 cohort from the client's own player-segmentation system; every clip proven to belong to a Segment 469 member.

Why it matters: unlike issues 1–7, this one stops the main progression line outright — it is the only filmed defect where the player cannot continue the game's core loop at all. It also produced the pilot's clearest player-initiated evidence: a support ticket filed on camera. Recommended sizing step: pull the error-state logs to count how many players are blocked, on which servers, for how long.

Examples:

  1. Arena recommendation list fails to generate → main quest blocked → support ticket filed in-session → logout.
  2. Same verified harvest, adjacent payment moments show the friction texture around it: a Monthly Gem Gift Pack taken to the payment modal then cancelled; a Companion Dummy attempt blocked by insufficient diamonds.
  3. Several Segment 469 sessions end with a payment confirmation popup still on screen — the session dissolves mid-checkout.
3.9

What the warehouse sees of all this (the contrast)

What billing/telemetry shows What it cannot show

purchase_failure telemetry events exist They carry no shop/offer/item context — 483 of 483 events (100%): which offer fails is uninstrumented
Nothing for issues 1, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 Walls, lying ad errors, server waits, crashes, ad friction and the Arena block leave no warehouse trace at all (no client-error/UX/telemetry table exists in the granted set)

The warehouse counts one issue family's footprint; the recordings turned it into eight named, fixable defects — two of which (silent success, lying ad errors) misreport reality to both the player and the data.
