Retention & Churn

Where new players drop off, how they behave before they quit — each proven on real recordings.

About the screens. Screens are reconstructions of moments observed in recorded sessions, with on-screen wording lightly reworded. Actual client footage is withheld to preserve anonymity.
Section 1

Where new players stop, and what holds them up

What this is

361 people were recorded from the moment they first installed Game-01. Every session was followed quest by quest through their first hundred guide quests.

That cap is the comparison window. It puts every player on the same stretch of the game, so time spent on a step, where someone got stuck, and how they went about it can be compared person to person.

A player counts as churned when the recordings stop and never resume: 281 players. A player counts as active when they were still being recorded when the capture window closed: 41 players. A further 39 installed too close to the end of the window to judge either way, and they are left out of comparisons.

Every number says what it is out of. Every claim rests on a specific second of footage.

Player loss spreads across the early experience, but the gap between churned and active users opens sharply across the first ten quests and widens again between quests 51 and 100.

  • No single early quest causes immediate mass attrition.
  • A combined 85 of 253 churned players stop before reaching quest 11, whereas 0 of 34 active players drop out in that span.
  • The pre-quest phase sees 48 of 267 churned players stop, and quest 1 sees 33 of 165 churned players leave.

The studio could soften early combat timers and reduce boss HP barriers between quests 51 and 100 to smooth progression walls.

The studio should not change early quest flow or combat systems, because churned players play identically to active players.

Most recordings end mid-progress when capture stops, but idle drop-offs and intentional exits mark real churn.

  • Footage ends during active play for 196 of 281 churned players and 39 of 41 active players. The capture tool stops clips automatically every fifteen minutes. Active players were filmed for longer, which explains their higher share.
  • Leaving the device running marks a real split. This idle state appears in 34 of 281 churned players and 2 of 41 active players. These players let the app drift into sleep mode and never return.
  • Early bounce claims 23 of 281 churned players and 0 of 41 active players. Their sessions were too short to record any gameplay journey.
  • Deliberate exits happen only among churned players. This exit behavior covers 17 of 281 churned players and 0 of 41 active players. For example, one player opened settings to confirm logout after repeated battle losses.
  • Screen blocks and dead ends affect only the churned group. There were 7 of 281 churned players stuck on screen and 4 of 281 churned players playing without advancing. Neither pattern appeared in the active group, with 0 of 41 active players for both.

The studio could add an alert before sleep mode triggers and streamline early onboarding to retain players who leave immediately.

  • Failing a fight is the leakiest loop, leaving 314 of 1280 encounters unresolved.
  • Boss encounters break combat recovery when players run out of time and walk away, as seen against Boss A and Boss B.

The studio can smooth the sharpest difficulty spikes in early chapter bosses and replace hard purchase popups with non-intrusive store prompts.

Problems for the development team

3.1

Problems worth fixing

Store errors block real money payments, session drops kick players out of combat, and early feature locks frustrate routine actions.

  • Store errors directly stopped players from completing real cash transactions. Payment checkout errors blocked purchases for 3 of 281 churned players and 1 of 41 active players. Vendor initialization errors stopped checkout for 2 of 281 churned players. Failed ad loads blocked free rewards for 4 of 281 churned players and 1 of 41 active players.
  • Sudden session expiration popups kicked players out during live combat. This disconnect struck 9 of 281 churned players and 1 of 41 active players. Battle verification mismatches forced 2 of 281 churned players out of active play. Indefinite screen hangs froze the client for 2 of 281 churned players.
  • Active players were recorded longer, so they had more time to hit errors.
  • Players tried to set custom names before the tutorial allowed it. Guide quest locks blocked name changes for 7 of 281 churned players and 2 of 41 active players. Name collisions forced 1 of 281 churned players to test many rejected choices.
  • Automated play halted when background limits were reached. Full equipment bags stopped auto combat for 1 of 281 churned players. Maximum health caps blocked potion upgrades for 1 of 281 churned players and 1 of 41 active players.

The studio should fix payment gateway timeouts, auto-dismantle overflow items during idle combat, and unlock name changes from the start.
