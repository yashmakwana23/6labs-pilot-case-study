What we add

Your data says what happened. The screen says why.

Your dashboards already give you the numbers. What they cannot give you is a reason, because the reason only ever existed on the player’s screen. Oracle plays the sessions back and names it, with the footage attached.

About the screens. Screens are reconstructions of moments observed in recorded sessions, with on-screen wording lightly reworded. Actual client footage is withheld to preserve anonymity.
1New-player churn
Where do new players hit friction?

BI answers this with a count. So many new players installed, so many never came back. What it cannot show is what they ran into on the way, because friction leaves no row in a table. The recordings surface those friction points one at a time, starting before the game has finished loading.

  • The first friction point is the sign-in screen. Four new players met a failure there on install day: a wrong password, a cancelled sign-in, a publisher account in protection mode.
  • The message names the cause, then leaves the player stuck. “ID or password is incorrect” and a code — 2001, 0006, 10006 — above a single OK. Knowing what went wrong is not the problem. There is no password reset, no account recovery, no next step of any kind. The only action the screen offers is to dismiss it and try the same thing again.
  • The recovery exists, just not inside the game. One player had to open the publisher’s website on a PC, confirm their identity and reset a password before they could get in. On install day, before a single minute of gameplay.
  • None of this exists in any table. A churn dashboard counts the player who left. Only the recording shows the screen that met them first.
2Monetization
What opens the wallet, and what blocks it?

Revenue reporting tells you a purchase happened, or failed. It cannot tell you which screen produced it. Oracle finds the session, plays it back, and shows the moment the wallet opened — and the moment the sale was lost.

  • This player had already spent USD 531 by day 29. They opened the pass, tapped buy, and the store refused before the payment sheet ever appeared.
  • The error fires instantly on tap, then the identical attempt frequently clears seconds later. That is a pattern consistent with a race condition in the store handshake, rather than connectivity or the player’s network.
  • All 5 players hit by it were active spenders. This is not lost conversion, it is revenue refused at the moment the customer had already decided to pay.
  • Warehouse reporting records the attempt as incomplete. It cannot say the store never opened, or that the same tap worked on retry.
3UX friction
Why do players stop trying?

A failure screen is where the game either teaches or loses someone. This one states the outcome and stops there. Nothing on it turns the loss into a next step.

  • The timer reads 00:00 and the boss still holds 42% of its health. The player can see they fell short. What the screen never does is turn that into a number they can act on.
  • No combat power target. No damage figure. No suggestion of what to raise. The screen offers Retry or Leave, so players retry the same fight unchanged and fail the same way.
  • A new player has no way to work any of this out on their own. Nothing on screen connects the failure to the system that would fix it.
4Progression locks
What is the game refusing, and why?

Players meet refusals that name a rule without naming what triggered it. This one blocks a first-day action behind a prerequisite it declines to identify.

  • The player typed a two-character nickname and was refused: “You have not finished the preceding guide quest.”
  • The message never names the quest, never says how far away it is, and never says what renaming has to do with quest progress. The quest tracker in the corner reads Guide Quest 25, but nothing connects the two.
  • The same dialog carries a second, unrelated meaning elsewhere: the name is taken, or contains a blocked word. One refusal, two causes, and no way to tell which one you hit.
  • Naming your character is one of the first things a new player tries. Here it is gated by something invisible, at the moment they are deciding whether the game is worth their time.
5Limits enforced too late
Why is the button still lit?

The row already reads its maximum, and the button beside it is still lit and priced. The screen offers an upgrade it is going to refuse, and only says so after the player has tapped it.

  • Both rows read stage 9 of 9 — and both Enhance buttons are still fully lit and priced, at 98,600 and 97,500 gold. A maxed-out control that still looks purchasable.
  • The refusal only arrives after the tap: “Recovery cannot be enhanced above Max HP or MP.” The button never greys out and never disables. The state is on the screen; the control ignores it.
  • That message appears in 613 recordings across the observed population. This is not one confused player, it is a control that invites an action it will refuse.
  • The missing piece is the same one as the nickname lock above: what raises this, and when. A greyed button naming the cap turns a wasted tap into a goal.
